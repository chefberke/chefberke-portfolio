A case study brief came in. I had never done one.

I read it a few times and wrote nothing. I did not know what a finished one was
supposed to look like, so I did not know where to begin. Before you start, everything
looks like that. Too big, and not yours yet.

This is not a post about how to do a case study. I still cannot tell you that. It is
about what happened when I stopped waiting to know.

## Close any decision

The first thing I did was pick the tools. It did not really matter which way it went,
and that is the only reason I could decide it.

When you are stuck at the start, the move is not to find the decision that matters. It
is to close any decision at all. Each one I closed made the next one smaller.

![Five squares in a row, each smaller than the one before it. The last and smallest is filled blue.](/images/blog/my-first-case-study/decisions.svg "each decision makes the next one smaller")

## Start with what you know

I did not start with the hard part. I built the ordinary parts first: the screens, the
buttons, the things you tap.

That was partly avoidance. But it put something real on a real screen, and the real
problem showed up the moment I touched it. I did not find it by planning. I walked
into it.

## The first number

When I finally checked how often it got the answer right, the first result was bad.
Close to a coin flip.

It did not feel good, but it was the most useful moment of the whole project. Until
then the project was a feeling. After that, it was a number I could work on, and I
pushed it up step by step.

Bad news is still news. Nothing tells you where you are until there is something to
look at.

## The point

I could not have planned any of this, and I do not think anyone plans their first one.
The order only got clear once I had started.

You do not need to know how before you begin. Decide something small. Build the easy
part. Look at the first number. You find out how by doing it.

The brief turned into an app I called viora, and it is on github.
